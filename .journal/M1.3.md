## What I did

- Ratified single daemon thread + `BoundedBuffer.poll(timeoutMs)` over `ScheduledExecutorService` (size trigger needs to fire on the Nth record's arrival, not on the next tick).
- Made `BatchSink` a `@FunctionalInterface void accept(List<LogRecord>)` with `BatchSink.NOOP` default.
- Decided empty intervals do NOT invoke the sink (strict reading of spec §2.3).
- Sink failures swallowed inside `flush()` for M1.3 — M1.4's `ResilientSink` takes over the no-loss contract; M1.3's job is just keeping the daemon alive.
- `close()` stops the flusher with no drain. Drain is C9 / M1.5.

## Problems I faced

- **Initial sketch had a busy-poll loop** (`Thread.sleep(10)` between `drainTo` calls). Cheap to write but burns CPU on idle SDKs and adds 10 ms median latency to the size trigger. Replaced with `BoundedBuffer.poll(timeoutMs)` — adding the poll method to the buffer was a 5-line change and the flusher loop got dramatically simpler.
- **`BeaconSdkEmitTest` broke once the flusher started auto-running.** The M1.2 tests asserted `sdk.buffer().size() == 100` after emitting 100 records, but with the new flusher that buffer is concurrently drained. Workaround: call `sdk.close()` post-build in those tests to stop the flusher before asserting. (M1.5 had to revisit this — see that journal.)

## What could have been done better

- **Should have looked harder at `BlockingQueue`'s existing API before sketching the busy-poll loop.** `poll(timeout)` is the textbook primitive for "wait up to N ms for a record or wake up early when one arrives" — exactly what the size+interval logic needs. Probably 20 minutes wasted on the wrong abstraction before backing out.
- **`BeaconSdkEmitTest`'s `sdk.close()` workaround was a quick fix that didn't account for the future close() drain in M1.5.** A clean solution would have been to expose the flusher directly to tests from the start (via `sdk.flusher()` accessor) and have those tests use `flusher().stop()`. Did surface in M1.5 and got fixed cleanly there, but the same instinct earlier would have saved one of M1.5's three test-refactor commits.
